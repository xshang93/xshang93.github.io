Research vision

Next-generation critical metal components through AI-driven manufacturing

Turbine blisks and other critical parts must run hotter, last longer and weigh less, often more than one alloy can deliver. Multi-material parts put the right alloy where it is needed, and AI can now design them. Two gaps stand between these designs and real parts:

  1. AI designs seldom consider manufacturing limits, so the parts can crack or form brittle phases.
  2. Additive manufacturing cannot yet place each material accurately where it was designed.

My lab, the Additive Innovation and Artificial Intelligence (AI²) Lab, will close both gaps.

Future research

Two research directions

1

AI design of manufacturable multi-material components

Generative AI, guided by surrogate models of phase formation and of the printing process, proposes designs that perform well and can be built without defects.

Builds on: bi-metallic steels, multifunctional multi-metal parts, AI inverse microstructure design.

2

AI-driven additive manufacturing for accurate material placement

Toolpath planning before printing, and AI closed-loop control with multi-modal sensors during printing, so that each material ends up where it was designed.

Builds on: a multi-material DED machine I built, AI process optimization, melt-pool studies, closed-loop DED.

About

Engineer, researcher, builder

I am a licensed Professional Engineer and a postdoctoral researcher at MIT working with Prof. A. John Hart and Prof. Zachary Cordero, supported by an NSERC Canada Postdoctoral Research Award. I earned my PhD in Materials Science and Engineering at the University of Toronto with Prof. Yu Zou.

My work sits where manufacturing processes, mechanics of materials and artificial intelligence meet. I build the machines, write the control and optimization software, and test the parts that come out.
