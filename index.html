Research vision

Overcoming the manufacturing bottleneck in AI-driven design

AI now designs new alloys and parts far faster than we can make them. My lab, the Additive Innovation and Artificial Intelligence (AI²) Lab, will make AI designs buildable, and AM able to build them.

{% include scales.html %}
Future research

Two challenges for my lab

1

Build manufacturability into AI design

AI designs rarely account for what a metal AM machine can build, or at what cost.

2

Make AM accurate and fast enough

So that AM can build what AI designs, to specification and in a certifiable way.

Ongoing projects

Current, unpublished work at MIT and with collaborators.

MIT · DARPA METALS

Functionally graded turbine blisk

A component-level, multi-alloy gas-turbine blisk, planned with a toolpath–composition optimizer that accounts for dilution and heat build-up.
MIT · Mechanosynthesis Group

Hybrid inkjet + laser powder bed fusion

Jetting nanoparticle inks onto each powder layer to alloy metals voxel by voxel and print smooth in-plane composition gradients.
U of T · In preparation

Deep-learning closed-loop control of DED

A learned controller that reads images of the build and adjusts the laser on the fly.
UCL · Synchrotron X-ray

Magnetic fields as a melt-pool actuator

Direct X-ray evidence of thermoelectric magnetohydrodynamic flow in DED of 316L and IN718, toward a new knob for melt-pool control.

About

Engineer, researcher, builder

I am a licensed Professional Engineer and a postdoctoral researcher at MIT working with Prof. A. John Hart and Prof. Zachary Cordero, supported by an NSERC Canada Postdoctoral Research Award. I earned my PhD in Materials Science and Engineering at the University of Toronto with Prof. Yu Zou.

My work sits where manufacturing processes, mechanics of materials and artificial intelligence meet. I build the machines, write the control and optimization software, and test the parts that come out.
