Research vision

Overcoming the manufacturing bottleneck in AI-driven design

AI now generates new alloys, architectures and parts far faster than any human team, and many of the most promising designs, especially functionally graded parts, are best made by additive manufacturing (AM). Manufacturing has become the bottleneck, from two sides. My planned Additive Innovation and Artificial Intelligence (AI²) Lab will remove it from both.

Challenge 1

AI design ignores manufacturability

Generative and agentic AI push performance, but rarely account for what a metal AM machine can build, or at what cost.

1.1

Process- and cost-aware AI design

Process and cost models constrain geometry and composition while the design is generated, not afterwards.

AI: generative design + uncertainty-aware process models
1.2

Composition-aware toolpath planning

Plans where each material ends up, how heat builds up and how long the build takes, not just geometry.

AI: multi-objective Bayesian optimization + ML surrogates + agentic AI
Challenge 2

AM cannot build AI designs accurately or quickly enough

AI completes a design iteration in hours, while building the part still takes weeks to months.

2.1

Transferable AI process-optimization models

Models that carry over to new compositions, machines and AM processes with few new experiments.

AI: physics-informed learning + transfer learning + active learning
2.2

Adaptive AI closed-loop control for graded AM

Controllers that track the planned composition, not just melt-pool geometry, and correct it in real time.

AI: learned adaptive control + multi-modal sensing

Cost and qualification are design objectives alongside performance in every project: the goal is parts that perform well and are also affordable and certifiable.

Ongoing projects

Current, unpublished work that lays the groundwork for the four projects.

MIT · Toward Project 1.2

Functionally graded turbine blisk

Characterizing the manufacture of graded blisks and planning their toolpaths, accounting for dilution and heat build-up.
U of T · Toward Project 2.2

Deep-learning closed-loop control of DED

A learned controller that reads images of the build and adjusts the laser on the fly. Manuscript and patent application in preparation.
MIT · Graded materials by LPBF

Hybrid inkjet + laser powder bed fusion

Jetting nanoparticle inks onto each powder layer to alloy metals voxel by voxel and print smooth in-plane composition gradients.
UCL · Synchrotron X-ray

Magnetic fields as a melt-pool actuator

In-situ X-ray imaging of DED of 316L and IN718 under a magnetic field, toward a new input for melt-pool control. Manuscript in preparation.

About

Engineer, researcher, builder

I am a licensed Professional Engineer and a postdoctoral researcher at MIT working with Prof. A. John Hart and Prof. Zachary Cordero, supported by an NSERC Canada Postdoctoral Research Award. I earned my PhD in Materials Science and Engineering at the University of Toronto with Prof. Yu Zou.

My background spans AI-driven design and process optimization through to building and improving metal AM machines. I build the machines, write the optimization and control software, and test the parts that come out.
