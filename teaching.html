Teaching & mentoring

Learning by designing, building and testing

The best engineering lessons I have learned came from building something that did not work the first time. I want my students to get those lessons too: to connect theory to hardware, to use data and AI with judgement, and to take responsibility for the safety and quality of what they make.

Teaching approach

Design–build–test. Two of my most productive student teams built real research hardware: a group of Master of Engineering students and I designed and built a full-size metal 3D printer, and a multidisciplinary capstone team added machine-learning-based monitoring and control to it. I will bring the same approach to the classroom through projects that end in a physical test.

Data and AI as engineering tools. Having been a teaching assistant for courses on data science and AI for materials engineers, I teach students to ask what a model has learned, where it will fail, and how to check it against physics and experiment.

Professional practice. As a licensed Professional Engineer with industry-facing service in SME, I link course material to codes, qualification and the realities of manufacturing.

Mentoring

I have mentored more than 20 Master of Engineering and undergraduate students, mostly in team projects that built research hardware.

Fall 2023 – Winter 2024

Capstone: autonomous DED system

Led a team of 5 undergraduate students from Electrical & Computer, Materials, and Mechanical Engineering in APS490 Multi-Disciplinary Capstone Design to develop an autonomous DED system using machine learning and computer vision. This work fed into our closed-loop DED platform, which later won 1st place in the ASM IMAT Materials Venture Challenge.

Fall 2021 – Summer 2022

MEng project: building a metal 3D printer

Led a team of 4 Master of Engineering students from Materials and Mechanical Engineering in an MSE MEng project to design and build a full-size hybrid laser metal 3D printer from scratch. The machine became the DED platform behind much of my PhD research.

Mentoring beyond the lab. Since 2020 I have organized The Martlets Society, a non-profit run by early-career researchers that hosts talks on research and careers by researchers from leading universities (see Service & Outreach).
