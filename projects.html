Functionally graded gas-turbine blisk

A blisk (bladed disk) needs different properties in different places: strength and fatigue resistance at the bore, different performance at the rim and blades. With Prof. Zachary Cordero, I am designing, fabricating and testing a component-level blisk whose alloy changes radially (IN718 → INCOLOY 909 → Monel K-500), built by multi-material DED.

  • Built a planner that co-optimizes the toolpath and the composition for the whole blisk, accounting for dilution between tracks and heat build-up.
  • Groundwork for Project 1.2, composition-aware toolpath planning, whose first target is a blisk graded from a fatigue-resistant bore to creep-resistant blades.

Hybrid inkjet + laser powder bed fusion

Graded metals made by DED are limited to millimetre resolution, and powder bed fusion has so far graded composition only layer by layer. In Prof. A. John Hart's group I am developing a hybrid process that jets nanoparticle-loaded inks onto each powder layer before the laser melts it, so the alloy forms in situ, voxel by voxel.

  • Goal: smooth in-plane composition gradients at powder bed fusion resolution.
  • A heated build plate supports crack-prone alloys such as refractory metals for propulsion and energy.

Magnetic fields as a melt-pool actuator

A static magnetic field adds a new force inside the melt pool: thermoelectric currents at the solid–liquid boundary interact with the field and drive flow exactly where the microstructure forms. With Prof. Peter D. Lee at University College London, I used in-situ synchrotron X-ray imaging to watch DED of 316L stainless steel and IN718 under a magnetic field.

  • Direct evidence of thermoelectric magnetohydrodynamic (TEMHD) flow in DED.
  • Next: use the field as an extra control input for melt-pool shape, dilution and microstructure.

Deep-learning closed-loop control of DED

Open-loop DED drifts as heat builds up in the part. On the DED platform I built at the University of Toronto, a deep-learning model reads images of the build and adjusts laser power on the fly to keep each track on target.

  • 1st Place, ASM International IMAT Materials Venture Challenge (2025).
  • Funded in part by a Connaught Innovation Award (2023).

Radial bi-metals under ultra-high-speed impact

Strength and ductility usually trade off, and the trade-off is harshest under impact. Building on the confined-necking mechanism, I designed radial bi-metallic architectures made by DED that overcome the strength–ductility trade-off under ultra-high-speed impact.

  • Presented at the 155th TMS Annual Meeting (2026).

Published projects

Custom multi-material directed energy deposition (DED) platform

Laboratory for Extreme Mechanics & Additive Manufacturing, University of Toronto

I led the design and construction of a full-size DED system from scratch. It has a 1 kW IPG fiber laser, two powder hoppers for multi-material deposition, and a user-friendly control interface, and it prints 316L and 17-4PH stainless steels, nickel superalloys and more. A CCD camera and a high-speed infrared camera monitor the melt pool in situ.

This platform produced the heterostructured bi-metallic steels and multifunctional parts below. On it we are also developing an AI-powered closed-loop controller that reads images of the build and adjusts laser power on the fly (unpublished; see ongoing projects).

Recognition: 1st Place, ASM International IMAT Materials Venture Challenge (2025); Connaught Innovation Award (2023). Manuscript and US patent application in preparation.

AIDED: accurate inverse process optimization for laser DED

Laboratory for Extreme Mechanics & Additive Manufacturing, University of Toronto

Finding good process parameters for DED, and for additive manufacturing in general, is slow and expensive, yet it decides build quality, cost and material waste. AIDED uses machine learning to predict melt-pool and track geometry from process parameters, and a genetic algorithm to search inversely for the parameters that give a target geometry, from single tracks to multi-track layers and multi-layer builds. It finds optimal parameters for given objectives within 1–3 h, and transferred from 316L to pure Ni with one-fifth of the data.

Shang, X., Talbot, A., Li, E., Wen, H., Lyu, T., Zhang, J., & Zou, Y. (2025). Accurate inverse process optimization framework in laser directed energy deposition. Additive Manufacturing, 102, 104736.   Code

Confined necking and improved tensile ductility in heterostructured bi-metallic steels

Laboratory for Extreme Mechanics & Additive Manufacturing, University of Toronto

Using DED, we made a new type of mesoscale heterostructured material: bi-metallic steels that combine 316L and 17-4PH stainless steels, which have very different strength and ductility. By tuning the ratio and arrangement of the two steels, we improved tensile ductility, ultimate tensile strength and yield strength over 316L by 27.3%, 11.5% and 1.8%, respectively. The gains come from (i) confined-necking co-deformation, where 316L confines and delays the necking of 17-4PH and gives the whole sample more work hardening; (ii) a switch between necking-induced and shear-induced failure; and (iii) hetero-deformation-induced (HDI) hardening.

Shang, X., Dash, S. S., Shao, C., Lang, L., … & Zou, Y. (2026). Confined necking and improved tensile ductility in heterostructured bi-metallic steels made by additive manufacturing. Acta Materialia, 121952.

Additive manufacturing of multi-metallic materials for multifunctionality

Laboratory for Extreme Mechanics & Additive Manufacturing, University of Toronto

Multifunctional materials perform several useful functions without complex joints. Metallic ones are attractive for their strength and durability but hard to design and make, because several metals must be integrated during manufacturing. DED can deposit different alloys exactly where they are needed. With carefully programmed toolpaths, we demonstrated four multifunctional metal parts: (i) tunable magnetic lifting force, (ii) information embedded securely and durably as a material fingerprint, (iii) improved ferrofluidic seals, and (iv) programmable mechanical properties. Two US patent applications arose from this work.

Shang, X., Li, E., Dash, S. S., Talbot, A., & Zou, Y. (2026). Additive manufacturing of multi-metallic materials to achieve multi-functionality. MetalMat, 3(1), e70032.

Tailoring the mechanical properties of 3D microstructures

Laboratory for Extreme Mechanics & Additive Manufacturing, University of Toronto

Designing a material for a specific application requires capturing complex process–structure–property relationships. Finding microstructures inversely from target mechanical properties is especially hard because the design space is so large. We built an end-to-end framework that handles both forward prediction and inverse search: a 3D convolutional neural network predicts stress–strain response from a 3D microstructure, and a genetic algorithm searches for microstructures that meet target properties, cutting the time from target properties to a candidate microstructure.

Shang, X., Liu, Z., Zhang, J., Lyu, T., & Zou, Y. (2023). Tailoring the mechanical properties of 3D microstructures: A deep learning and genetic algorithm inverse optimization framework. Materials Today, 70, 71–81.   Code

Mechanism and quantification of melt-pool morphology in laser DED

Laboratory for Extreme Mechanics & Additive Manufacturing, University of Toronto

Melt-pool shape sets track geometry, dilution and defects in DED. Combining computational fluid dynamics with in-situ high-speed infrared imaging, we explained how the melt pool evolves during single-track deposition and quantified its morphology, the kind of process model that feeds the design and control work in my research plan.

Zhang, J.*, Shang, X.*, … & Zou, Y. (2025). Mechanism and quantification of melt pool morphology evolution in single-track fabrication by laser directed energy deposition. Journal of Manufacturing Processes, 156, 556–568. *Co-first authors.

Durable bistable auxetics made of rigid solids

Pasini Group, McGill University

Bistable auxetic metamaterials (BAMs) are perforated periodic structures with a negative Poisson's ratio. Under tension, a BAM expands to a second stable state through a large shape change, which normally relies on a soft elastomer; made from a rigid polymer or a metal, its ligaments rupture. We extended the BAM architecture to rigid base materials and used experiments and simulations to assess performance, bistability and durability over 10,000 cycles. Geometric maps show how the main architectural descriptors control behaviour, opening BAMs to a wide range of materials, including elastic–perfectly plastic and potentially brittle ones.

Shang, X., Liu, L., Rafsanjani, A., & Pasini, D. (2018). Durable bistable auxetics made of rigid solids. Journal of Materials Research, 33, 300–308. Featured in MRS Bulletin. US Patent 10,767,032.
